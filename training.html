Day 01

IBB & Promoter Fundamentals

Understand IBB, your role, campaign structure and professional expectations.

1. What is IBB?

Indian Business Bureau connects businesses with people through structured campaigns, tasks and opportunities. The promoter community helps execute approved campaign activities according to instructions.

Simple meaning: IBB organizes campaigns. Businesses have marketing needs. Promoters complete assigned activities according to the campaign brief.

2. Who are the three sides?

  • Business: Provides the campaign requirement.
  • IBB operator/manager: Communicates with the business and organizes the campaign.
  • Promoter: Receives assigned tasks and completes them correctly.

3. What does a promoter actually do?

  • Receive a task or campaign brief.
  • Understand exactly what is required.
  • Create or publish approved content where instructed.
  • Follow deadlines.
  • Submit genuine proof.
  • Report completion.
  • Ask the IBB operator when something is unclear.
Important: Promoters should not independently contact IBB clients/business owners unless IBB management specifically instructs them to do so. Client communication is handled by the IBB operators.

4. Professional behavior

  • Never claim work was completed when it was not.
  • Never submit fake or manipulated proof.
  • Never make promises on behalf of a business.
  • Never change campaign instructions without approval.
  • Keep communication respectful.
  • Ask questions before making assumptions.
Example: A salon campaign asks you to create one 15-second vertical reel showing a service. You receive the brief from IBB, create the content according to the instructions, publish/submit it as required, and send the required proof back to IBB.

Day 02

Understanding Campaigns & Tasks

Learn how to read a campaign brief before doing any work.

1. What is a campaign?

A campaign is a structured marketing activity with a specific objective, instructions, deliverables and completion requirements.

2. Read the brief before starting

Every task should be understood through these questions:

  1. What is the business?
  2. What is the objective?
  3. What exactly must I create?
  4. Where must it be posted/submitted?
  5. What deadline applies?
  6. What proof is required?
  7. Are there words, claims or formats I must avoid?

3. Deliverables

A deliverable is the actual work expected from you: for example one photo, one reel, a story, a caption, a screenshot or another specified activity.

Pro habit: Never start creating content before you know the deliverable, deadline and proof requirement.
Example brief:
Business: Local Gym
Task: 1 vertical reel
Length: 15–30 seconds
Content: Show environment + equipment + experience
Proof: Published reel screenshot/link
Deadline: As specified by IBB

Day 03

Mobile Photography Fundamentals

Learn how to capture clean, useful and professional-looking photos.

1. The five basics

  1. Light: Use sufficient, clean light.
  2. Focus: Make the important subject sharp.
  3. Composition: Keep the subject intentionally positioned.
  4. Background: Remove unnecessary distractions.
  5. Stability: Avoid accidental camera shake.

2. Lighting

Whenever possible, use soft natural light. Avoid placing the subject directly under extremely harsh overhead lighting. Check the screen before taking the shot.

3. Angles

Try eye-level, slightly elevated and detail shots. The correct angle depends on the subject and campaign objective.

4. Before & after

If a campaign requires a transformation, capture both states consistently. Keep framing and conditions as similar as practical so the comparison is clear.

Do not digitally manipulate a result in a way that misrepresents the actual business, product or service.

Day 04

Reels & Short-Form Video

Learn the structure of a simple, watchable vertical reel.

1. What makes a useful reel?

  • Clear subject
  • Strong opening
  • Simple sequence
  • Readable text where required
  • Good lighting
  • Clean audio where relevant
  • Clear ending or call-to-action when instructed

2. The first few seconds

The opening should immediately show the viewer what the video is about. Avoid spending the beginning on unnecessary blank footage.

3. Simple reel structure

  1. Hook — introduce the subject.
  2. Context — show where/what it is.
  3. Experience — show the useful or interesting part.
  4. Detail — add supporting shots.
  5. Ending — CTA or conclusion if required by the brief.
Shoot vertically when the campaign calls for short-form vertical content. Keep the phone stable and record more footage than you think you need so you have options while editing.

Day 05

UGC & Authentic Content

Learn how to make content that feels natural without making false claims.

1. What is UGC?

UGC means user-generated content. It generally presents a product, service or experience in a natural, user-oriented format.

2. Simple UGC structure

  1. Problem or situation
  2. Introduction to the experience
  3. What happened / what was shown
  4. Specific observations
  5. Conclusion or CTA when required

3. Talking video

Speak clearly, keep sentences short and focus on the campaign message. Do not invent personal experiences you did not actually have.

Avoid unsupported claims such as guaranteed results, guaranteed income, medical promises or statements you cannot verify.

Day 06

Editing Like a Professional

Learn the basic mobile editing workflow.

1. Basic editing workflow

  1. Import your footage.
  2. Remove unusable clips.
  3. Trim unnecessary beginnings/endings.
  4. Arrange clips logically.
  5. Add text/captions when required.
  6. Add suitable audio or voice-over.
  7. Check the entire video.
  8. Export using the campaign's required format.

2. Keep transitions simple

Transitions should support the content rather than distract from it. Clean cuts are often enough.

3. Captions and text

  • Keep text readable.
  • Do not cover important visual information.
  • Check spelling.
  • Keep text on screen long enough to read.
Before submitting, watch your entire exported video once without editing it. You may notice spelling, timing, audio or framing problems that were missed during editing.

Publishing & Content Quality

Pre-publish checklist

Platform differences

The same creative may need different captions, dimensions, text placement or publishing treatment depending on the platform and campaign instructions. Always follow the specific campaign brief.

Do not use copyrighted material, misleading claims or content that violates the applicable platform's rules or the campaign's instructions.

Day 08

Proof, Verification & Reporting

Learn how to prove that assigned work was actually completed.

1. What is proof?

Proof is evidence that allows IBB to verify completion of the assigned task. The required proof depends on the campaign.

2. Common proof types

  • Screenshot of the published content
  • Content link where applicable
  • Before/after evidence when specifically requested
  • Required task screenshot
  • Other evidence specified in the campaign brief

3. Good reporting format

Task: Campaign name
Status: Completed
Deliverable: 1 reel
Published/submitted: Yes
Proof: Screenshot/link
Notes: Any relevant issue
Fake, edited or misleading proof can result in task rejection or account action according to applicable IBB rules. Always submit genuine evidence.

Day 09

Professionalism, Reliability & Work System

Build the habits that make campaign execution dependable.

1. A reliable promoter

  • Checks tasks regularly.
  • Understands instructions before starting.
  • Accepts only work they can realistically complete.
  • Communicates problems early.
  • Meets deadlines.
  • Submits accurate proof.
  • Maintains respectful communication.

2. When you make a mistake

  1. Do not hide it.
  2. Stop if continuing could make the issue worse.
  3. Inform the IBB operator.
  4. Explain what happened clearly.
  5. Follow the correction instructions.

3. Daily promoter routine

  1. Check available tasks.
  2. Read the full brief.
  3. Confirm requirements.
  4. Plan the work.
  5. Create content.
  6. Quality-check.
  7. Publish/submit as instructed.
  8. Save proof.
  9. Report completion.
Remember: campaign availability, eligibility, task volume and any applicable incentive depend on actual campaigns and IBB terms. Training does not by itself guarantee a particular amount of earnings.
